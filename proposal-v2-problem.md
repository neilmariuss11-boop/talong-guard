## 1. THE PROBLEM

### 1.1 The Pest

The Eggplant Fruit and Shoot Borer (EFSB), *Leucinodes orbonalis* Guenée (Lepidoptera: Crambidae), is the most economically destructive pest of eggplant (*Solanum melongena*) in South and Southeast Asia (Cork et al., 2003; Srinivasan, 2008). Female moths oviposit on leaves and fruit surfaces at night; newly hatched larvae bore into shoots and fruits within hours, feeding internally where no contact insecticide can reach them (ISAAA, 2020).

**The fundamental challenge:** Damage is invisible until the larva has already destroyed the fruit from the inside. By the time a farmer observes entry holes or frass, the produce is reject-grade.

### 1.2 Scale of Damage in the Philippines

Eggplant is the Philippines' leading vegetable crop in both area and volume:

| Indicator | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Area harvested | 14,000+ hectares | PSA, 2023 |
| Annual production | 248,000 metric tons | PSA, 2022; Statista, 2023 |
| Production value | ₱1.027 billion (2024) | CEIC |
| Yield loss from EFSB | 51–73% at high pressure | Hautea et al., PLOS ONE, 2016 |
| National annual economic loss | ₱33.85 billion | ISAAA, 2021 |

Sources: [PSA](https://psa.gov.ph/vegetable-root-crops/eggplant) · [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/philippines/production-value-agriculture-current-price/production-value-crops-eggplant) · [Hautea et al., 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0157498) · [ISAAA, 2020](https://www.isaaa.org/blog/entry/default.asp?BlogDate=9/23/2020)

### 1.3 Current Farmer Practice: Blind Spraying

Filipino eggplant farmers rely almost exclusively on calendar-based insecticide application — spraying every 2–3 days regardless of actual pest presence, accumulating **60–80 spray applications per cropping season** (UPLB-CAFS; ISAAA, 2020). In Bangladesh, this figure reaches up to 180 applications per year (Cornell Bt Eggplant Partnership).

This practice is:

**Expensive.** Pesticide costs consume **20–40% of total production cost** (ISAAA, 2021), representing ₱20,000–50,000 per hectare per season — often the farmer's single largest expense after labor.

**Ineffective.** Despite intensive spraying, yield losses remain 51–73% because the larvae are inside the fruit, beyond the reach of contact insecticides (Hautea et al., 2016). Spraying kills beneficial natural enemies but rarely the target pest at the right life stage.

**Dangerous to health.** A study of eggplant farms in Pangasinan, Philippines found that 63.8% of farmers experienced skin irritation, 29.3% eye redness, 27.6% muscle pains, and 27.6% headaches related to pesticide exposure. A maximum of 20% of eggplant fruit samples tested positive for insecticide residues (Sangchan et al., 2014). Broader studies in Benguet Province found 63.3% of vegetable farmers experiencing muscle pains and 55% muscle weakness (Lu, 2009).

**Environmentally destructive.** Soil samples from 42% of eggplant farms in the Pangasinan study tested positive for insecticide residues, six exceeding maximum allowable limits (Sangchan et al., 2014).

Sources: [Sangchan et al., 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12199-014-0425-3) · [Lu, 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2778185/) · [ISAAA, 2020](https://www.isaaa.org/blog/entry/default.asp?BlogDate=9/23/2020)

### 1.4 Why Better Solutions Haven't Reached Farmers

Three pathways could potentially solve the EFSB problem. All three are blocked:

#### A. Genetic Resistance (Bt Eggplant) — Court-Banned

Bt eggplant (*Solanum melongena* expressing Cry1Ac from *Bacillus thuringiensis*) demonstrated near-100% efficacy against EFSB in Philippine field trials, with potential to increase marketable yield by 192% and reduce pesticide use by 48% (Hautea et al., 2016; ISAAA, 2020).

However, the Philippine Supreme Court issued a **Writ of Kalikasan** (environmental protection writ) on April 19, 2024, ordering a **cease-and-desist on all commercial propagation, field testing, and related activities** for Bt eggplant and Golden Rice (SC, 2024). The Court of Appeals applied the precautionary principle, citing "conflicting scientific views and uncertainties on risks and effects" (CA, April 17, 2024).

This writ has **no fixed expiration.** Precedent: a writ of kalikasan against mining operations in Palawan has been active for 15 years and remains pending (Mongabay, 2021). Given the legal process, anti-GMO advocacy strength, and subsequent seed production and distribution timelines, Bt eggplant is unlikely to reach Filipino farmer fields within the next **10–15 years**, if ever.

**Filipino eggplant farmers cannot rely on genetic resistance. They need pest management tools that work with conventional varieties.**

Sources: [SC Writ](https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/sc-issues-writ-of-kalikasan-on-genetically-modified-rice-and-eggplant-products/) · [PANAP, 2024](https://panap.net/2024/04/victory-against-gm-golden-rice-and-bt-eggplant-in-the-philippines-court-a-momentous-win-in-the-global-fight-against-gmos/) · [Mongabay, 2021](https://news.mongabay.com/2021/04/hits-and-misses-for-a-legal-tool-to-protect-the-environment-in-philippines/)

#### B. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) — Proven but Poorly Adopted

An IPM package combining sex pheromone trapping, weekly shoot/fruit sanitation, and need-based spraying has been extensively validated in field trials across Asia:

- Fruit damage reduced from 41.8–51.2% (untreated) to **22–26.4%** in IPM plots (Cork et al., 2003)
- Fruit damage as low as **20.46%** with weekly shoot clipping + pheromone traps at 100/ha (JCP, 2017)
- **35–36% additional yield** over pesticide-only regimes (JCP, 2017)
- Profit margins and production area **significantly increased** for adopting farmers; pesticide use and labor **decreased** (Kabir et al., 2023)

**Yet adoption remains extremely low.** A study of 438 vegetable growers in Bangladesh (Kabir et al., PLOS ONE, 2023) found:

> **"27% of farmers who adopted pheromone traps abandoned them shortly after. Main reason: time-consuming to manage insect pests."**

Additional barriers identified:
- **Knowledge gap:** "Farmers had difficulty understanding the lifecycle of *Leucinodes orbonalis* and the pheromone trapping mechanisms, despite the IPM being technically and economically viable" (Srinivasan, 2008)
- **Pesticide dealer influence:** "Likelihood of dis-adoption was higher for farmers who maintained communication with private pesticide company agents" (Kabir et al., 2023)
- **No local lure supply:** Pheromone lures are not sold in Philippine agri-supply stores; all commercial lures are manufactured in India
- **Single-pest focus:** "The major focus in an IPM strategy is on a single major pest rather than on all pests that farmers would like to control" (Kabir et al., 2023)

Sources: [Kabir et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10540953/) · [Cork et al., 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-entomological-research/article/abs/female-sex-pheromone-of-brinjal-fruit-and-shoot-borer-leucinodes-orbonalis-lepidoptera-pyralidae-trap-optimization-and-application-in-ipm-trials/7B3B195A5566DC1E675FACB4A0365BD4) · [Srinivasan, 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238693090) · [JCP, 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317701467)

#### C. Manual Monitoring (Counting Moths) — Nobody Sustains It

The science behind threshold-based spraying is well established. Male EFSB moths are attracted to synthetic sex pheromone lures, and entomologists have established that **6–8 moths per trap per night constitutes the Economic Threshold Level** requiring intervention.

But manual monitoring requires a farmer to:
1. Walk to the field every morning at dawn
2. Open the trap
3. Count and identify small moths (~20mm wingspan)
4. Record the number
5. Compare to threshold
6. Decide whether to spray
7. Replace lure every 3–4 weeks
8. Repeat daily for 3–4 months

**No smallholder farmer sustains this.** The 27% dis-adoption rate documented by Kabir et al. (2023) reflects this labor burden directly.

## 2. THE GAP

The problem is not the absence of science. **The science is settled.** Pheromone-based monitoring with threshold-driven spraying decisions, combined with cultural sanitation practices, demonstrably reduces fruit damage by 35–50% while cutting pesticide use by half or more.

The problem is a **chain of translation failures** between laboratory knowledge and farmer practice:

```
RESEARCH proves IPM works
    ↓ but...
EXTENSION system fails to deliver knowledge to farmers
    ↓ and...
TOOLS (manual traps) require too much labor — 27% quit
    ↓ and...
LURE SUPPLY doesn't exist in Philippine retail
    ↓ and...
PESTICIDE DEALERS actively undermine IPM adoption
    ↓ and...
GENETIC SOLUTION (Bt eggplant) is court-banned
    ↓ therefore...
FARMERS SPRAY BLIND — 60-80x/season, ₱20-50k wasted, health damaged
```

**The gap is not knowledge. The gap is a practical, low-labor, accessible system that translates proven IPM science into farmer action.**

No product currently fills this gap in the Philippines:

| Existing Option | Why It Fails the Gap |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Manual pheromone trap | Too labor-intensive; 27% dis-adoption rate |
| Pest ID apps (Plantix, etc.) | Detect damage AFTER it occurs; require smartphone + internet |
| Commercial smart traps (Trapview, iScout) | ₱48,000–56,000; designed for large-scale Western agriculture |
| Calendar spraying | No intelligence; maximum cost, minimum efficacy |
| Bt eggplant | Court-banned indefinitely |
| Extension worker visits | Infrequent (monthly or less); cannot provide daily monitoring data |

## 3. PRELIMINARY FIELD CONVERSATIONS

### Disclosure on Methodology

Preliminary conversations (3–5 individuals) were conducted by team members with eggplant farmers in Mindoro as part of early problem exploration. These conversations were initially framed around **post-harvest detection** (identifying borer damage in harvested fruit), reflecting an earlier research direction (NIR-based detection). The interview instrument may have contained **leading elements** that oriented respondents toward detection-focused responses.

**We do not claim these conversations validate our specific solution.** We cite them only for what they reliably captured: **confirmation of problem severity and farmer pain.**

### What the Conversations Confirmed (Problem-Level)

The following themes emerged consistently and are independently corroborated by published research:

| Theme from Conversations | Research Corroboration |
|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Farmers discover borer damage only after fruit is visibly compromised or at harvest | Consistent with EFSB biology — larvae bore internally within hours of egg hatch (Cork et al., 2003) |
| High percentage of harvest is reject-grade due to borers | Consistent with 51–73% yield loss data (Hautea et al., 2016) |
| Farmers express frustration at cost of pesticides with poor results | Consistent with 20–40% production cost on pesticides (ISAAA, 2021) |
| No farmer mentioned or recognized pheromone traps or monitoring-based strategies | Consistent with knowledge gap findings (Srinivasan, 2008; Kabir et al., 2023) |

### What the Conversations Did NOT Validate

- Willingness to pay for a specific device at a specific price point
- Willingness to change spraying behavior based on SMS alerts
- Acceptance of pheromone-based monitoring as a concept

**These must be validated through properly designed interviews using non-leading instruments aligned with our actual solution direction (see Validation Plan).**

## 4. SOLUTION DIRECTION: AUTOMATED IPM SYSTEM

### Rationale (Research-Justified)

The research evidence points to a clear design brief:

1. **Pheromone monitoring + sanitation + need-based spraying works** — 35–50% damage reduction, proven across multiple trials (Cork et al., 2003; JCP, 2017)
2. **The labor burden of manual monitoring is the #1 adoption barrier** — 27% quit because "time-consuming" (Kabir et al., 2023)
3. **Farmers lack the knowledge to interpret monitoring data** — lifecycle understanding is missing (Srinivasan, 2008)
4. **No affordable automated alternative exists** — commercial smart traps cost ₱48,000–56,000
5. **No genetic alternative is available** — Bt eggplant is court-banned (SC, 2024)
6. **The pheromone lure supply chain doesn't exist in PH** — all lures are imported from India

### Design Requirements Derived from Research

| Requirement | Justification |
|-------------|--------------|
| Must automate moth counting | Eliminate #1 dis-adoption reason: labor (Kabir et al., 2023) |
| Must deliver actionable alerts, not raw data | Farmers don't understand moth counts or threshold meaning (Srinivasan, 2008) |
| Must work on basic phone (SMS) | Smallholder farmers in Mindoro use budget phones; internet unreliable |
| Must be solar-powered, autonomous | Reduce daily maintenance burden to near-zero |
| Must include IPM guidance beyond "spray/don't spray" | Sanitation reminders address the full IPM package, not just monitoring |
| Must cost <₱5,000 | Commercial alternatives at ₱48–56k are inaccessible to smallholders earning ₱80–200k/year |
| Must filter false moth counts | Accuracy must exceed 80% to build farmer trust (validation KPI) |
| Must include lure supply pathway | No PH retail source exists; device without lure is useless |

### Proposed Solution: TalongGuard

An automated, solar-powered pest monitoring and IPM coaching system that:

1. **Attracts** EFSB moths using species-specific sex pheromone lure
2. **Counts** moth entries using multi-layer IR sensing (physical size filtering + beam-break duration filtering + dual-beam direction detection)
3. **Decides** by comparing nightly count against Economic Threshold Level (6–8 moths/trap/night)
4. **Alerts** farmer via SMS: actionable instruction ("Spray today" / "Safe, save your money")
5. **Coaches** farmer on complementary IPM practices via timed SMS (shoot removal reminders, lure replacement scheduling, harvest quality tracking)
6. **Supplies** pheromone lures as consumable refills (imported, with long-term roadmap to local sourcing partnerships)

### What Is Novel

| Element | Novelty Claim | Basis |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Automated moth counting at <₱5,000 | No product exists at this price point in PH or SE Asia | Market survey; commercial alternatives 10–15x more expensive |
| Multi-layer IR filtering (physical + duration + dual-beam) | Novel approach to species-discrimination without camera or visible light | Engineering design; avoids light-attraction contamination inherent in camera-based systems |
| SMS-based IPM coaching system | No system delivers timed, crop-stage-aware IPM practice reminders to Filipino farmers' phones | Extension service gap documented by Kabir et al. (2023) and Srinivasan (2008) |
| Integrated lure supply chain for PH market | First consumer-accessible EFSB pheromone lure supply in the Philippines | No retail channel currently exists |
