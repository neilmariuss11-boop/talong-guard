## SLIDE 2: THE PROBLEM

### Filipino eggplant farmers are losing ₱33.85 BILLION per year to a single pest.

**The Eggplant Fruit and Shoot Borer** (*Leucinodes orbonalis*)

- Eggplant = #1 vegetable crop in the Philippines (21,225 hectares)
- Borer larvae destroy **51-80%** of the harvest
- By the time farmers SEE damage, fruit is already reject-grade
- Farmers spray pesticides **up to 80 times per season** — blindly
- Pesticide costs eat **20-40%** of total production cost

**The real problem: farmers have NO way to know WHEN pests are coming.**

*[Visual: split image — healthy eggplant vs. bored eggplant. Farmer spraying in field.]*

**Speaker note:** "Imagine spending ₱30,000 on pesticide every season, spraying every other day, and STILL losing half your crop. That's the reality for eggplant farmers across the Philippines."

---

## SLIDE 3: THE INSIGHT

### The moths that cause the damage are detectable BEFORE they lay eggs.

- Male EFSB moths are attracted to **sex pheromone lures**
- Scientists know the exact threshold: **6-8 moths/trap/night = time to act**
- Below threshold = no spray needed, save your money
- Above threshold = spray TODAY before eggs hatch

**The science exists. The affordable technology to deliver it to farmers does not.**

Until now.

*[Visual: moth lifecycle diagram — adult moth → eggs → larva inside fruit. Arrow: "INTERCEPT HERE"]*

## SLIDE 4: THE SOLUTION

### TalongGuard: Smart pheromone trap with automated moth counting + farmer alerts.

**How it works:**
1. Pheromone lure attracts EFSB moths into funnel trap
2. Infrared sensor automatically counts each moth
3. Onboard computer compares nightly count to scientific threshold
4. SMS alert sent to farmer's phone:
   - "Safe — no spray needed tonight" = **save money**
   - "Threshold reached — spray now" = **save your crop**

**Solar-powered. Works in the field. No internet needed — uses SMS.**

*[Visual: product render/photo of prototype. Diagram of trap-to-phone alert flow.]*

**Speaker note:** "TalongGuard replaces guesswork with data. Instead of spraying 80 times hoping for the best, farmers spray only when the science says they need to."
