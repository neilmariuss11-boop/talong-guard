## SLIDE 3: WHY CURRENT SOLUTIONS FAIL

| What farmers do | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| **Spray insecticides** (305 of 310 farmers [R21b]) | Harabas is **resistant**: up to 2,477-fold to diamides [R43] |
| **DA pheromone traps** | They only **count** moths. They don't reduce them |
| **Spray *Metarhizium*** | **Sunlight kills the spores within hours** [R25] |

### The gap: nothing *reduces* the moth population without spraying.

**Speaker notes:** "The government already puts pheromone traps in onion fields [R16, R22]. Those traps attract exactly the right moth, the male harabas, and then do almost nothing with him."

## SLIDE 4: THE INSIGHT

### Don't catch the moth. **Arm him.**
- Each male harabas mates with about **5 females** [R33].
- He flies straight to them.
- If he carries a **slow-acting natural fungus**, he infects every mate.
- **The pest becomes the sprayer.**

*[Visual: 1 male moth → 5 females → egg masses → larvae, with the fungus spreading at each step]*

**Speaker notes:** "This is called autodissemination. It's published science. It has been demonstrated for fall armyworm, diamondback moth, false codling moth, thrips and whitefly [R4–R9]. Nobody sells it for harabas."

---

## SLIDE 5: THE PRODUCT

### [BRAND] Station
1. **Lure** (commercial *S. exigua* pheromone) attracts males at night.
2. Moths hit the **baffle vanes** and drop into a **velvet tube dusted with *Metarhizium anisopliae*** spores.
3. They **exit alive** through the open bottom, **carrying the fungus**.
4. **No power, no spraying.** Swap the spore sleeve every **2 weeks** and the lure every **4 weeks**.

*[Visual: exploded view — 40 cm hood, lure cage, baffle, 20 cm PVC tube, velvet sleeve, pole]*

**Speaker notes:**
- The spores sit **in the dark**. That solves the sunlight problem that kills sprayed *Metarhizium*.
- The lure is more than 20 cm from the spores by design.
- It looks like the trap farmers already know. The difference is that nothing gets caught, and that's the point.

## SLIDE 6: WHY IT WORKS: THE EVIDENCE CHAIN

| Step | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Harabas males come to the lure in PH onion | UPLB field studies [R13, R14]; DA uses it now [R16] |
| Moths pick up dry spores from velvet devices | icipe devices [R5, R6]; commercial powder stations [R32] |
| Moth pheromones don't block the fungus | Fall armyworm, false codling moth [R4, R7] |
| Contaminated *Spodoptera* moths die (~4 days) and **infect their mates**; fewer and less viable eggs | Akutse et al. 2020 [R4] |
| The fungus kills harabas larvae | NCPC-UPLB isolates [R1]; Korea FT83 [R10]; OMSC **San Jose** field trial [R15] |

### The pieces are proven. The product doesn't exist yet.

**Speaker notes:** "We chose *M. anisopliae* over *M. rileyi*. Only *M. anisopliae* has data on adult moths, and it can be mass-produced with PhilRice's rice protocol [R11]. *M. rileyi* is our version 2 with NCPC."
