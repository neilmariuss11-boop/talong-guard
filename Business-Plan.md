# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The problem:**
- Onion armyworm (*Spodoptera exigua*, **harabas**) causes the biggest pest losses in Philippine onion:
  - ₱1.61 B in Nueva Ecija in 2016 [R12].
  - 12,138 ha infested nationally in 2024 [R16].
  - ₱30 M in damage in Occidental Mindoro in 2024, with San Jose under a state of calamity [R17].
- Farmers answer with insecticides the pest increasingly resists [R43].
- DA pheromone traps only count moths.
- *Metarhizium* sprays die in sunlight within hours [R25].

**The solution:** the [BRAND] Station, a passive, pole-mounted autodissemination device.
1. A commercial sex-pheromone lure attracts male harabas moths at night.
2. The moths drop through a shaded velvet tube dusted with *Metarhizium anisopliae* spores.
3. They leave alive to infect the females they mate with.
4. Infected moths die in about 4 days, infected females lay fewer and less viable eggs [R4], and the fungus kills larvae [R1, R10].

No power, no spraying. A spore sleeve is swapped every 2 weeks and a lure every 4 weeks.

**The market:**
- Occidental Mindoro, the fastest-growing onion province: 8,637 ha in 2026 [R18]. Beachhead: San Jose (~3,285 ha [R19]).
- Expansion: Nueva Ecija (~11,500 ha [R36]).

**The business:**
- The Station sells for **₱690** (59% margin).
- A per-Station **Season Kit** (3 lures + 6 spore sleeves) sells for **₱520** (37% margin).
- For the farmer: **₱2,080/ha/season** after the first season, about **0.8% of onion gross value**.
- First customers: **LGU and DA crop protection programs**, which already buy harabas pheromone traps [R22], plus irrigators' associations and commercial growers.

**Traction plan:** 20+ interviews, a bench test, and a field demonstration on 3–5 San Jose farms (Nov 2026 – Mar 2027), with a field day and pre-orders.

**Financials:**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ₱41,400 | ₱767,600 | ₱2.70 M |
| Net | −₱170,100 | −₱47,800 | **+₱393,360** |

Cumulative positive in Year 3.

**Ask:** ₱180,000 pilot funding, endorsement letters, lab access, and approval of the demonstration.

# 3. PROBLEM STATEMENT

## The pest
- *Spodoptera exigua*: nocturnal moth; larvae defoliate onion.
- Males find females by pheromone and mate about 5 times each [R33].
- Moth catches in PH onion peak 25–59 days after planting [R13].
- Outbreaks can arrive by long-distance migration [R12].

## The scale of damage

| Event | Impact |
|---|---|
| Nueva Ecija 2016 | 5,330 ha; 4,089 farmers; ₱1,609,997,316 [R12] |
| National 2024 | 12,137.92 ha infested; 674 ha totally damaged [R16] |
| Occidental Mindoro 2024 | ₱30 M; San Jose, Magsaysay, Looc state of calamity [R17] |
| Pangasinan outbreak | Inputs ~₱400k/ha vs ~₱200k normal; about half the crop saved [R21] |

## What farmers do today
- **Spray:** 305/310 farmers during infestations [R21b]. Resistance is high: diamides up to 2,477-fold, emamectin [R43].
- **Monitoring traps from DA:** they help timing (1–3 sprays instead of weekly [R13]; ~40% fewer sprays [R14]) but **don't reduce populations**.
- **Biocontrol sprays:** effective in the lab and locally [R1, R15], but **UV kills spores within hours** [R25].

## The core problem
There is **no affordable, labor-light tool that reduces harabas populations without spraying**.

# 4. SOLUTION

## What it is
A pole-mounted Station: a 40 cm white hood with a lure cage, baffle vanes, and a 20 cm PVC tube lined with a **removable velvet sleeve dusted with 2 g *M. anisopliae* conidia**. The bottom is open so moths exit alive.

## How it works
1. The lure attracts male harabas at night.
2. They hit the baffles, drop through the velvet tube, pick up spores, and exit.
3. They mate and pass spores to females [R4].
4. Adults die in about 4 days (LT50 3.9 d [R4]); egg-laying and hatch fall [R4, R39]; larvae are killed by the fungus [R1, R10]; cadavers produce new spores [R11].

## Key features
- **Spores protected from the sun:** opaque tube, 40 cm hood and a sun collar; the velvet gets direct sun only when the sun is below ~29° elevation.
- **Lure kept away from spores:** ≥21 cm, beyond the published precautions [R5, R6].
- **No power, no spraying, no smartphone.**
- **2-minute servicing:** slide-in sleeve, snap-in lure.
- **Looks like the familiar DA trap**, so it is easy to adopt.

## Value proposition
> **"Ang gamu-gamo mismo ang magdadala ng lunas."** *(The moth itself carries the cure.)*
> Fewer emergency sprays, a non-chemical mode of action against resistant harabas, and an area-wide effect, for ~0.8% of onion gross value per season.

| | Insecticides | DA monitoring trap | *Metarhizium* spray | Mass trapping | **[BRAND]** |

| Reduces population | Larvae | ✗ | Larvae | Some | **Adults → eggs → larvae** |

| Labor | High | Low | High | Low–medium | **Low** |

**Our moat:** first-mover relationships with the LGU and DA; a Utility Model on the Station design [R30]; a local spore supply chain; the first local demo data.

# 10. TECHNICAL OVERVIEW

## Architecture (passive)
Hood (Ø400 mm) → lure cage (adjustable) → 80 mm open entry window with cross baffles → 4-inch PVC tube (200 mm) with a 50 mm sun collar and a 150 mm velvet spore sleeve → open bottom → pole mount (tube bottom about 600 mm above soil).

## Specifications

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Lure | Commercial *S. exigua* rubber septum; 4-week replacement [R23] |
| Agent | *M. anisopliae* dry conidia, 2 g per sleeve; 14-day replacement [R5] |
| Lure–spore distance | ≥ 21 cm |
| Sun exposure of velvet | Only when the sun is below ~29° elevation |
| Density | 4 per ha |
| Power | None |
| COGS | ₱285 (Station), ₱20 (cartridge) |

Full design, BOM, fabrication SOP, QC and tests: `phase5-device-design.md`.

# 16. RISK ANALYSIS

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low spore pickup in the Station | Medium | High | Bench test first; variants A1–A3, C, D |
| Effect not visible to farmers in one season | Medium | High | Position as prevention; field-day evidence; spray logs |
| FPA barrier for cartridges | High | High | Staged pathway; partner registrant or government program |
| Low humidity limits infection [R10] | Medium | Medium | Measure night RH; v2 formulation with a partner |
| Spores die in heat or UV | Medium | Medium | White, shaded, ventilated design; 14-day swaps |
| Lure cost or customs | Medium | High | 2 suppliers; bulk; freezer |
| Farmers expect "caught moths" | High | Medium | Education; demo |
| Low-price glut years | High | Medium | B2G; pay-at-harvest |
| Non-target effects | Low | Low–medium | Species-specific lure; point source [R28, R40] |
| Theft or damage | Medium | Low | Cheap unit; cooperative ownership |
